# Population regulation: evidence and calibration
## Research references (checked 2026-11-26)
- WHO Housing and Health Guidelines (2018), Household crowding, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK535289/ and systematic review https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK535299/ : close-contact infectious disease evidence, heterogeneous measures (persons/room, floor area), household crowding distinct from geographical density.
- Shi et al., RSV-associated acute lower respiratory infection risk factors in under-fives, systematic review/meta-analysis (2015/2017), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26682048/ : crowding pooled odds ratio 1.94 (95% CI 1.29–2.93). Disease/age/outcome-specific association, not a per-contact probability.
- Virus Watch prospective community cohort, England and Wales, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38807847/ : overcrowded vs under-occupied households adjusted odds ratio for positive SARS-CoV-2 PCR 2.45 (95% CI 1.43–4.19). Modern context, not direct prehistoric calibration.
- Olofin et al. (2013), pooled 10 prospective studies of under-five growth and mortality, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23734210/ : severe wasting all-cause mortality hazard ratio 11.63 (95% CI 9.84–13.76) compared to reference Z score >= -1. Chronic measured anthropometric status, not momentary hunger, and hazard ratio is not death probability.
- WHO malnutrition fact sheet, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/malnutrition : undernutrition increases vulnerability to disease and death.
- Davenport (2020), Urbanization and mortality in Britain c.1800–50, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7186836/ : historical urban mortality disadvantage with heterogeneous disease and sanitation conditions; not universally inevitable.

## Implemented provisional translation
Use a bounded 1–2x additional odds adjustment for local crowding conditional on actual exposure, roughly anchored to the order of magnitude of household-study associations. This is an original tuning assumption, NOT the published effect estimated for a pulse, and may double-count contact effects if not calibrated; compare with contact-only baseline. Neighbors <=2: 1x; 3:1.25x; 4:1.5x; 5:1.75x; >=6:2x. Tile-neighbor thresholds are arbitrary engineering proxies pending dwellings/physical scale. Example base p=20% becomes 33.33% at 2x odds, not 40%. No source means no transmission. Acute need stress 60–100 adds up to 100% disease damage; this is a bounded placeholder, not a numerical translation of severe-wasting HR. Existing health/mortality and resource transactions remain authoritative. No global population death penalty.

## Required calibration before release
1. Benchmark identical seeds, same infectious contacts, low/high local occupancy; measure exposure, incidence, secondary attack proportions, disease-specific recovery and fatalities. Include mixed immunity and no-source controls.
2. Compare contact-only vs additional crowding model; avoid double-counting. Once structures exist, measure actual occupied space/ventilation, not raw tile count, and separate close-contact, waterborne and other transmission routes.
3. Track age-specific deaths, infant and under-five mortality, birth intervals, age-specific fertility, disease case fatality, starvation, chronic nutrition, clean water and housing access. Convert annual or period-specific probabilities to simulated-time hazards using explicit time units; use odds conversion only for odds ratios. Select era- and setting-appropriate historical references with uncertainty.
4. Compare resource-poor/rich settlements, sanitation interventions, density, seasonal shortages and population collapse/recovery across seeds. No forced convergence to a population target. Real-world data calibrate distributions, not guaranteed individual outcomes.
5. Validate performance and long-run histories after storage redesign. Temporary 254 live-descendant and 512 lifetime identity limits still exist; neither is a natural population limit. No claim that the current simulation supports unrestricted population.
